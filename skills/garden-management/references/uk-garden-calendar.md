# UK garden year — month by month

Calibrated for a temperate maritime UK garden (Herts / south-east of England, RHS hardiness
H4–H5): last frost around **mid-May**, first frost around **late October**, mild wet winters,
rain shadow dry spells in late summer. Shift timings a week or two for milder/colder sites and
always check the actual forecast before recommending an outdoor job.

## Task titles: the plant page already says which plant

A task attached to a plant (`--subject plant:N`) is shown **on that plant's page**, under its name.
Title it as the work, not as the plant: "Cut off the yellow leaf", not "Swiss cheese plant: cut off
the yellow leaf". The subject does the joining; a repeated name reads as noise, and the app strips
it on the plant page anyway — so a padded title just makes the stored row disagree with the screen.

- ✅ `task add --title "Move it off the radiator" --subject plant:1 --due 2026-10-03`
- ❌ `task add --title "Swiss cheese plant: move it off the radiator" --subject plant:1`
- In a **cross-plant** summary (weekend plan, monthly review, Telegram) name the plant yourself, once
  per task or by grouping — the title no longer carries it.

**Before you apply any month below to a specific plant, check `placement`.** This is a calendar of
outdoor timings: frost, planting out, hardening off, staking, birds and hedgehogs belong to
`placement='outdoor'` plants only. Indoors there is no frost, no rain and no nesting season — an
indoor plant takes from these months only the light, watering, feeding-season and pest-watch items,
and its own frost date is the day the heating goes on. A plant with no `placement` recorded gets
asked about (or the indoor reading), never the frost one.

## January
- Structural pruning of dormant trees/shrubs (not Prunus, not evergreens — wait for late spring for those).
- Winter prune apples/pears (not trained forms); start rose pruning after Christmas if mild.
- Plan the year: seed orders, crop rotation, bed layout (`task add`, `rules add`).
- Tender pots: move off wet ground, wrap, keep off the cold. Check stored dahlia/gladioli tubers.
- Pond: keep a hole open in any ice by resting a pan of hot water on it — never smash it (shock kills fish).
  No feeding below ~8–10 °C. Filters off/frost-protected.

## February
- Finish winter pruning; cut back deciduous grasses and *Cornus*/*Salix* stools for coloured stems.
- Divide snowdrops/daffodils *after* flowering, not before.
- Mulch fruit bushes; top-dress beds with well-rotted compost (peat-free).
- Chit first early potatoes; sow broad beans, hardy salads under cover.
- Pond: watch for frog spawn; start thinking about pump/filter restart; first water test if mild.

## March
- First feeds of the season on established plants (slow-release granular, or liquid as growth starts).
- Prune shrub roses, *Buddleja* (hard, to a framework), *Hydrangea paniculata*, *Caryopteris*, late-summer perennials cut back to the ground.
- Plant out/sow: hardy annuals, sweet peas, onion sets, early carrots, parsnips.
- Lawn: first cut on a high setting; edge; moss/weed treatment only if needed.
- Pond pump and filter back on; begin skimming. First water tests as activity resumes.

## April
- Mow weekly (raise the blade in dry spells; never scalp). Feed the lawn.
- Plant out potatoes, plant perennials; divide crowded herbaceous perennials.
- Deadhead daffodils but leave foliage 6 weeks; stake tall perennials early.
- Aphid watch on roses/soft growth; encourage ladybirds rather than spraying.
- Pond: water test weekly now; blanketweed watch begins as water warms; add oxygenators if needed.

## May
- **Hardening off** then planting out tender veg/flowers after the last frost (mid-May here).
- Watch for late frosts: fleece handy for tender growth and blossom.
- Watering regime starts for containers (daily in warm spells); mulch to lock in moisture.
- Thin fruit; tie in climbers; pinch out tomato sideshoots once established.
- Pond: algae will spike with sunlight + nutrients — shade, oxygenators, no tap-water top-ups in sun.
  Fish feeding resumes fully as water exceeds ~10 °C.

## June
- Deadhead weekly to keep flowers coming; cut back early perennials for a second flush.
- Water deeply but less often on established beds; containers daily.
- Trim hedges lightly (birds should have finished nesting — check before cutting).
- Sow biennials (wallflowers, foxgloves) and autumn/winter brassicas.
- Pond: weekly tests, watch herons if you keep fish, net falling blossoms, keep water level up with rainwater.

## July
- High summer: water, deadhead, stake, tie in. Harvest daily.
- Summer-prune trained apples/pears and *Wisteria* (long whips to 5–6 buds).
- Do not feed high-nitrogen; switch to potassium/tomato-type feeds for flowering/fruiting.
- Keep an eye on red spider mite in heat; damp down paths/greenhouse.
- Pond: oxygen lowest at dawn in hot weather — aeration/water movement matters; top up with rainwater only.

## August
- Cut lavender and other grey-leaved herbs immediately after flowering (do not cut into bare wood).
- Trim evergreen hedges (formal ones last this month for a tidy winter).
- Divide bearded irises; plant out winter salads; start collecting seed.
- Let the lawn breathe in dry spells; last chance for a summer feed on the lawn (autumn feed comes in September).
- Pond: debris and dying weed — remove excess before it decomposes and spikes nutrients.

## September
- **Autumn lawn feed** (high potassium, low nitrogen) — the most valuable feed of the year.
- Plant spring bulbs (daffodils first, tulips last); sow green manures on empty beds.
- Plant new trees/shrubs/hedging while the soil is warm; keep watering until established.
- Tidy tired perennials but leave seedheads for birds where they look good.
- Pond: net the pond before leaf fall; remove excess weed; watch for herons as water clears.

## October
- Bare-root planting season starts (and often the best window here); plant roses, trees, soft fruit.
- Cut back faded herbaceous growth, tackle congested crowns, mulch the beds.
- Clear fallen leaves from lawn and **especially from the pond** (a few leaves poison a small pond).
- Last lawn cut on a high setting; clean and service tools.
- Pond: netting on, leaves out, pumps start to come off at the end of the month in cold snaps.

## November
- Leaf clearance ongoing; insulate outdoor taps; drain hoses.
- Plant tulips (as late as possible to reduce disease); prune acers/*Betula*/*Vitis* only if needed (bleeding risk — late summer is better for acers).
- Protect tender perennials/pots: fleece, dry mulch, moved under cover.
- Order seed; inspect stored crops; check stakes and ties before winter gales.
- Pond: leave a leaf net on, keep a pump moving water but protect from frost, do not feed fish.

## December
- Structural pruning of dormant deciduous trees/shrubs; rose pruning can begin after Christmas.
- Tidy, sharpen tools, review the year in the registry: what flowered badly, what died, what to move.
- Net/brushing on fruit trees and roses against pests (winter washes for fruit trees).
- Keep feeding the birds (they help with pests all year); avoid feeders over the pond.
- Pond: ice watch, no feeding, no breaking ice, keep an air hole, check for unhappy fish/amphibians.
